Can CTG predict low arterial pH?

We tested how well models can identify low umbilical arterial pH from fetal heart rate and uterine activity recorded during labor.

What we examined

Cardiotocography (CTG) records fetal heart rate and uterine activity. We investigated whether these two signals carry enough information to rank recordings by the likelihood of umbilical arterial pH below 7.10, the endpoint used in these experiments.

We used the selected, single-center CTU-UHB database. In the 441-recording development split, models were fitted and evaluated in five folds. All windows from a recording stayed together. We kept 111 recordings separate and unscored. The windows end at the end of the recording, and the study did not establish a fixed lead time before delivery.

What we tested

Waveforms and numerical features
A small one-dimensional convolutional neural network (CNN) on the signals, plus logistic regression and XGBoost on derived features.
Decider and language-model readouts
Frozen Decider configurations, trained readouts, and separately fitted adapters such as Kev. We measured ranking and checked how alert thresholds traded off missed events and false positives.
CTG plots as image inputs
Decider on plots rendered from the waveforms, including JPEG compression and rotation with noise. The image tests recorded whether each plot could be read.

What we found

Conventional signal models provided a strong development benchmark. The initial waveform CNN reached 0.799 AUROC. Results below come from separate protocols on the same development cohort, so direct model comparisons need matched inputs and evaluation.

With a cutoff selected inside each training fold to target a 20% false-positive rate, Decider detected 14 of 45 events on the full window and 19 of 45 with the sixteen views. The fixed zero-margin cutoff raised zero alerts in both conditions.

In a separate Kev adapter experiment, AUROC was 0.542 and 0.565 for the two inputs. Both fixed 0.5 conditions detected zero events. Training with sixteen views involved sixteen times as many input presentations per recording.

All 441 clean rendered plots met the image readability checks. Real phone photos introduce capture conditions that remain to be tested.

Study registry coverage and limits

All 192 registry entries have at least four measured result rows at their reported scopes. Of those, 191 have four rows on the full 441-recording development split. The remaining study has four partial results on 177 recordings, with further training cancelled.

The development split was reused across experiments. External validation, mother-level independence, and clinical performance remain open questions.

What we’ll build

Next is a research prototype for offline CTG image analysis on a phone. It will compare a Decider readout with conventional ML on the extracted signals.

  1. Capture and digitize

    Turn a photographed or exported chart into two waveforms. Show which segments were read and flag images that are too incomplete to score.

  2. Compare on the same inputs

    Evaluate Decider alongside a waveform CNN or feature model. Measure extraction error, missed events, false positives, and how often the prototype declines to score.

  3. Test offline and validate independently

    Measure latency and memory on physical devices. Reserve separate data for evaluation before considering any clinical use.
