02Protocol

One recording, one outcome.

Cardiotocography (CTG) records fetal heart rate and uterine activity during labour. The question is whether these signals can flag umbilical arterial cord-blood pH below 7.10 at birth. Exactly 7.10 counts as negative. The study does not diagnose hypoxia or brain injury.

02 / Validation

Which recordings were scored?

One hospital in Brno contributed 552 selected recordings to CTU-UHB. Recordings were split before making windows, so all views of a recording stayed together.

The registry repeatedly compares methods on those same 441 recordings. One extra event caught changes sensitivity by 2.22 percentage points.

03 / Threshold example · #44

What did ten extra detections cost?

The same 30-minute SmallCNN scores were read at two cutoffs. TRAIN20 targets 20% false positives in training; it does not guarantee 20% on other recordings.

SmallCNN development outcomes at two decision thresholds
CutoffLow pH detectedFalse positives
Default 0.525/45 55.6%54/396 13.6%
TRAIN2035/45 77.8%116/396 29.3%

10 more events caught, 62 more false alerts. Ranking did not change: AUROC was 0.7995 at both cutoffs.

Which IDs have full-development results?

192/192 registry IDs have at least four measured rows at their reported scope. 191/192 have four across the full 441-record development set. The exception, #46, covers 177 recordings (18 events), not all five folds.

An ID can be a model, a decision rule, a replica or an alias. These counts do not represent 192 independent models or replications.

What would move this beyond a retrospective benchmark?
  1. Freeze the model and cutoff before a single evaluation on the locked 111. A small reserved cohort limits the precision of sensitivity estimates.
  2. Test at another centre with a defined time before delivery and report both missed events and false alerts.
  3. Test the intended input and hardware. A waveform ONNX export is not evidence of accurate camera digitisation or offline phone performance. Those need separate tests on actual photos and devices.

These are next steps. No clinical deployment, intervention benefit or individual risk estimate is established.

Earlier baselines, language models and imaging experiments

Initial ranking baselines

  • Waveform CNN: 0.799 AUROC, 0.291 average precision.
  • Logistic regression: 0.792 AUROC, 0.354 average precision.
  • XGBoost: 0.793 AUROC, 0.289 average precision.

Language-model and imaging experiments

  • Frozen Decider #24: default threshold raised no alerts. Its training-selected threshold caught 14/45 on 30 minutes and 19/45 with sixteen views.
  • Kev #42: its fixed 0.5 threshold detected no events in either arm. In the original matched-feature test, adapted Kev had a higher (worse) Brier score than calibrated logistic regression: 0.09158 vs. 0.08021.
  • Rendered plot readability is not diagnosis, extraction accuracy, or real phone-photo validation.

Next evidence needed: prespecify a model and threshold, then test on genuinely independent data. Offline deployment, real-image reliability, calibration, and clinical utility have not been established. Independence at the mother level was not established.

How to read the result rows
  • TP and FN partition the event count; FP and TN partition non-events. Sensitivity = TP ÷ (TP + FN); FPR = FP ÷ (FP + TN).
  • AUROC and average precision summarize ranking. Brier score and log loss evaluate probabilities; lower is better. A missing metric is not zero.
  • Reported cohort sizes and threshold policies vary. Select 441 for full-development rows and compare like with like. Tracker row numbers are provenance, not patient IDs.
